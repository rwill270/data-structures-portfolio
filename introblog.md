# Introductory Blog

In order to understand data science to the fullest extent, you also need to be able to understand history, ethics, and specific knowledge about the topic you are learning/coding about. Learning about data science in social and interdisciplinary contexts means you need to understand why the data is being collected, who it is for and impacts, and how the data was computed/coded. The most important challenges we face as a society today focus on inequality and educational access. We can model social topics and people to relate better to society as a whole, as we can make complex patterns and trends visible to the average human eye. We can further explore the data to uncover any hidden trends in these industries to possibly advise better policy systems and address points that are covered with problems. There are also many responsibilities that come with using data about people. Numbers don’t always capture the full context behind a trend. Bias is a huge problem we face with data, as data can sometimes reflect on past discrimination and inequality policies, which will ruin your model. There is also a privacy and security aspect to data, as everyone is allowed to consent on how their data is being used. What I am hoping to better understand in this course is how to solve real world problems that can have an impact on large populations. Learning who, what, when, where, why, and how are the key aspects to data science when making a model.
